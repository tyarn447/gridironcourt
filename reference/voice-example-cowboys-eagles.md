# Voice reference — House Cowboys at House Eagles

*A mock game with an invented result. Kept as the canonical example of the
voice described in VOICE.md. Not a real dispatch; not published to the site.*
---

## Chapter One: The Bargain

> *We Were Great Once.*
> — the words of House Cowboys

There are two kinds of men in this realm.

There is the one they put on the banners. Silver star at his throat, jaw like
a verdict, an arm that can put a ball forty yards downfield on a rope while
falling backward. He is the Lord of Cowboys and he is, objectively, the most
beautiful thing on this field.

And then there is the other one.

Shorter. Thicker through the shoulders. Green, not silver. He does not point
at the crowd. He does not point at anything. He is the Lord of Eagles and
he has spent the last four years quietly taking everything Dallas was
promised.

Tonight they meet on Philadelphia's ground, and I would like to say I came here
neutral.

Reader, I did not.

---

**The first half belongs to Cowboys,** and gods, they are lovely about it.
Long marches. Elegant, unhurried, the kind of football that gets a house on
primetime eleven years running with nothing in the vault to show for it. Their
lord throws, and the ball hangs, and seventy thousand people in green make a
sound like a wound opening.

Twenty to seven. He points at their crowd.

Something in my chest does something unforgivable.

**And then the second half,** where Eagles stops being polite.

No more deep throws. No more flourish. They take the ball and they *walk* —
four yards, four yards, four yards, an entire house pressed shoulder to
shoulder behind one man, and there is something almost indecent about how much
they enjoy it. This is not seduction. This is a house closing a door behind you
and taking its time.

Seventeen unanswered.

---

**Fourth down. Two yards. Seven minutes left.**

The kicker starts onto the field.

He waves him off.

He doesn't look at the sideline. He doesn't look at the clock — he *has* the
clock, he has had it since the second quarter, he has been carrying it around
this whole time like something he intends to give back later. He steps to the
line. He puts his hands under his center.

He has a look he gives the play clock when it drops under five, and I need you
to understand that I am describing a man in forty pounds of armor doing
arithmetic, and that it is the single most compelling thing I have witnessed
this year.

The ball is snapped.

He falls forward into six of his own men and they *carry him* —

Two yards.

That's the game. Seven minutes still on the clock and that's the whole thing,
it just takes Cowboys a while to admit it. **Twenty-four, twenty.**

---

**What it means, and I say this with love:** Dallas is 0–1 and will spend
this entire week being told their beautiful lord cannot win the games that
matter. It is the eleventh consecutive year of this. They will believe again
by Thursday. It's the most romantic thing about them.

Philadelphia is fine. Philadelphia was always going to be fine. Philadelphia knows it.

---

**The line you say tomorrow —**

> *"They lost it on the tush push. Again."*

Say it to any Cowboys bannerman. Say nothing after. The silence does the work.

---

*Ships of the week:* the Lord of Eagles and his center. It's not subtext.
*Villain of the week:* whoever keeps telling Dallas's lord he played fine.
*Next week:* Cowboys rides north, where it is cold and no one is impressed.
