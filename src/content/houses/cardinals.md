## The Wound

This is the oldest continuously operating house in the sport. It began in
Chicago in 1898. It has one championship, from 1947, won in a city it left
in 1960 for another city it left in 1988.

A hundred and twenty-eight years. One title. It is the longest sustained
disappointment in American sport and the realm barely registers it, which
is somehow the worst part.

## Four O'Clock

At precisely four in the afternoon, Eastern time — the exact minute the new
league year opened, not a minute into it — this house released
**Kyler Murray**.

The timing was not an accident. Nothing about it was. Murray was the first
overall pick in 2019 and the entire estate was rebuilt around him for seven
seasons: the offense, the marketing, the future. Then it wasn't working, and
both sides knew it wasn't working, and the parting was arranged so precisely
that it happened on the first tick of the clock that permitted it.

He wanted it this way. That detail is the one that stings. A trade would
have sent him wherever the houses agreed; a release let him choose. He
signed in Minnesota the following day.

And Arizona is still paying him. Roughly fifty-five million in dead money,
spread across two years by designation — about forty-seven and a half this
season, the balance next. This house will spend more on a man playing in
Minnesota than most houses spend on anyone.

Seven years, one plan, and it ends with a scheduled announcement and a bill
that arrives for two more winters.
