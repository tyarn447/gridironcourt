## The Wound

This house has won twice, and both times it was the Watch that did it — a
defense so violent it became the house's entire personality for a generation.

Then they found a lord who could do things no one had seen, gave him two
MVPs, and discovered the cruellest arrangement available: that you can have
the best player in the realm and still go home in January. Repeatedly. In
increasingly humiliating fashion, often at home, often as the favorite.

Last year they went 8–9 and missed everything. The realm has them third
going into this one, which tells you how little anybody believes that record.

## The Lord

**Lamar Jackson.** Twice the finest player in the sport by acclamation. Still
carrying the one thing acclamation cannot buy, and increasingly aware that
the window for buying it is a physical object with edges.

## The Knights

- **Trey Hendrickson** — on the Watch, a hundred and twelve million dollars,
  sixty guaranteed. The largest bet this house has made on a defender in
  years, and a return to the thing Baltimore has always actually been.

## Ancient Grudges

- **House Steelers.** The most physically unpleasant rivalry in the realm and
  the one both houses would keep if they had to give up all the others.
- **House Browns.** The original sin — this house *is* the old Cleveland
  team, relocated. Cleveland has never forgiven it and never will.

## What Changed This Spring

They spent enormously on the Watch: **Hendrickson**, **John Simpson**,
**Calais Campbell**. After a losing season, Baltimore's answer was to rebuild
the thing that won them their titles. It is either clarity or nostalgia.

## Prophecy

That 8–9 was an accident and everyone will be reminded of that shortly.
