> *We Believe Anyway.*
> — the words of House Jets

Eleven years ago a man in this house asked **Geno Smith** for six hundred
dollars.

It was a plane ticket for a camp Smith had missed because someone close to him
had died. Smith would not pay it. The man broke his jaw in the locker room, in
August, and was gone by that evening — and so, in every way that mattered, was
Smith. He lost the seat. He never got it back. He spent the next decade
elsewhere becoming exactly the thing this house had decided he was not.

They traded for him in the spring. He is thirty-six.

On Sunday he walked into a hall in Tennessee and won a game for the New York
Jets, which he had not done as a starter since the year after they gave up on
him.

---

The other reunion was on the far sideline and it was not a warm one.

**Robert Saleh** ran House Jets for three and a half years and was thrown out
five games into a season, which is the kind of firing a man does not get to
explain. Tennessee hired him in January to end four straight losing years. This
was his first day.

Both houses won three games last season. Nobody was watching this. It was, on
paper, the least important afternoon of the week: two of the poorest houses in
the realm, in front of a crowd with no reason to expect anything.

So they made it about ground.

**Breece Hall** — whom this house paid a great deal to keep, the one decision
nobody in New York argues about — carried it twenty-two times. A squire named
Sadiq, a young knight who is not supposed to run at all, took the ball moving
sideways on the very first march of the day and ran it in himself. First score
of his life.

---

Tennessee kicked early and led, briefly, and then something quietly terrible
happened to them.

They did not score again for eight consecutive attempts.

Not because of one calamity. Because the ball kept not being theirs. New York
held it for close to two-thirds of the afternoon — a house with nothing to prove
simply keeping the thing away from a house that needed it, march after march,
until the clock had eaten the entire argument.

**Cam Ward** was taken first overall in the realm a year ago and Tennessee has
spent real money trying to give him help. On Sunday he could not move anything
at all. His house finally scored with the result long settled.

There was no single disaster to point at afterward, which for a house at the
bottom is worse than a disaster. Nothing broke. It simply never started.

Smith is thirty-six and has not lost. Saleh lost his first day to the house
that threw him out.

*Ships of the week:* Smith and the building that broke his jaw, cautiously reconciled.
*Villain of the week:* whoever wanted his six hundred dollars back.
*Next week:* New York tries to do it twice, which is historically where this house struggles.
