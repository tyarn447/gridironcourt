> *Skol, And Suffer.*
> — the words of House Vikings

The rental lasted a quarter of an afternoon.

**Kyler Murray** came to Minnesota on a twelve-month lease. Not a succession
plan, not a future — a year, explicitly temporary, signed the day after Arizona
threw him out. In the first quarter of the first game he ran a yard, got hit by
two men at once, and did not come back. One of them was flagged for how he did
it.

So the seat was open, and everyone in that building knew who was supposed to be
standing behind it.

**J.J. McCarthy** was taken tenth overall two springs ago and told he was the
future of this house. He is twenty-three. He has also spent most of those two
years hurt — a knee that cost him an entire season, then an ankle, then a
concussion, then a small broken bone in his throwing hand — and four days
before this game Minnesota quietly moved him to third in line.

Third. Not the understudy. The understudy's understudy, in his own building,
watching a man hold the seat he was promised.

---

The man was **Carson Wentz**, who is thirty-three and was a lord once himself,
years ago, in another house, before a long and uncomplaining career of standing
in for other people.

Green Bay was ahead by twelve at the time. **Jordan Love** was having the kind
of day that usually settles these things, throwing for nearly four hundred
yards, and the Packers had every reason to feel the afternoon was theirs.

Then Green Bay came to a single stride on the ground, with one attempt left, and
sent Love himself to get it. He did not get it.

---

Minnesota scored, which brought them within two.

And here is where it turned, because a house that scores is then handed a small
certain point, a formality, the thing everyone takes. Minnesota declined it.
They stayed on the field to go for the extra one instead — two points, not one,
and two would put them ahead for the first time all day.

Wentz threw it to **Justin Jefferson**, who had already caught two scores from
him, and who caught this as well.

Ahead, for the first time, with everything still to play.

Green Bay did not score again. Minnesota won it comfortably in the end —
twenty-nine points in a row to finish, which is not a comeback so much as a
house remembering what it is in front of the one neighbour it cannot stand to
lose to.

And the lease is still twelve months. The lord is hurt, the heir is third in
line, and next Sunday somebody has to walk out of that tunnel first.

*Ships of the week:* Wentz and Jefferson, strangers in August, twice in an afternoon.
*Villain of the week:* the depth chart.
*Next week:* Minnesota decides, out loud, who it actually believes in.
