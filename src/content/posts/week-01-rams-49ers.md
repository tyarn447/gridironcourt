> *Five Rings And A Grudge.*
> — the words of House 49ers

Myles Garrett spent the morning on a cricket ground in Melbourne with a sleeve
on one leg, and he will not say which knee it is for.

This is the most destructive man in the sport. Los Angeles bought him in June
the way a house buys an ending — a knight handed over, three seasons of its own
future signed away, so that nobody would ever walk down a field on this house in
January again. He did not put a hand on anyone all morning. Afterward he said
the worst sentence a bought man can say: that out there, he had been thinking
about *himself*, and not about them.

---

The house had been in the country about thirty-six hours.

That was the plan — keep their bodies on California time, land,
sleep, win, leave. It worked in London last autumn, and Sean McVay is a clever
man, and this was a clever man's idea.

San Francisco had been there a week. Walking around in the sun. Letting the
clock become true.

And San Francisco is old. Everyone says so, always in that tone. Trent Williams
is thirty-eight; George Kittle is thirty-three, and so is Mike Evans, who
scored on his first morning in the colors. Deebo Samuel, sold off and brought home again,
scored too. Nick Bosa played on the knee that ended his last autumn, and Fred
Warner played on the ankle that broke and turned the wrong way in October — and
before the half was out, Warner had passed Patrick Willis, the man this house
has measured every other man against for twenty years.

Old is not the same as finished.

---

Out of the half Los Angeles finally went somewhere, arrived a yard short, and
had to choose: send a knight, or send the lord.

McVay sent the lord.

Matthew Stafford is thirty-eight, and seven months ago this realm named him the
best of all of them. He put his head down and went for the shortest distance in
the sport. Warner was there. So was a squire named Gracen Halton, who was
playing college football in December. They declined.

It looked, live, as though he had made it.

He had not.

San Francisco then took the ball the entire length of the field, unhurried, and
Purdy — running for his life outside the pocket — found Deebo alone in the end
zone. The enormous bowl understood.

Late, Los Angeles took its lord off the field. He was not hurt. A house arranged
this carefully does not spend an MVP on a lost morning, and Stetson Bennett came
out to run the clock down on something nobody wanted.

Los Angeles is still the favourite to win everything. It is still September.
But this house sold its whole future to be ready *now*, and then showed up a day
late.

> The Rams flew to Australia the day before and lost to a house that had been
> there a week.

*Ships of the week:* Purdy and Deebo Samuel, reunited, alone in an end zone on
the far side of the world.
*Villain of the week:* Raheem Morris, who built the Watch in Los Angeles and now
runs San Francisco's, and knows where everything in that house is kept.
*Next week:* Los Angeles goes home to explain itself.
